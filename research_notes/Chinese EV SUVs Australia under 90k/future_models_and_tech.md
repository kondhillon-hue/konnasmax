Research date: 11 November 2026. Method note: findings come mostly from web-search result summaries of Australian motoring outlets (Drive, CarsGuide, carsales, Chasing Cars, WhichCar, GoAuto, EV Central, Zecar, electrive, CarNewsChina). A direct fetch of CarExpert failed because the domain is blocked in this session's network settings, so CarExpert articles are cited from search-result titles and snippets only. Pricing is AUD before on-road costs (BOC) unless stated. "Confirmed" means the brand or its importer has said so on the record. "Estimate" means a media projection.

## 1. Which new Chinese SUVs are confirmed or rumoured for AU/NZ in late 2026 to 2027?

## 2. Battery tech roadmap (Blade 2.0, CATL, sodium-ion, solid-state, 800V/1000V, 5C to 10C)

### Takeaway
BYD's second-gen Blade (LFP, megawatt-capable) launched in China in March 2026. Sodium-ion is entering low-cost cars in 2026. Solid-state is only at pilot or small-batch stage around 2027, with mass rollout around 2030. For a buyer in 2027, the meaningful shift is faster charging (800V to 1000V, 5C+), not a big jump in range or chemistry.

### Cited Findings
- BYD unveiled the second-generation Blade Battery on 5 March 2026. With Flash Charging it claims 10% to 70% in five minutes, 10% to 97% in nine minutes, and 20% to 97% in about 12 minutes at -30°C — [Battery-News](https://battery-news.de/en/2026/03/06/byd-unveils-second-generation-of-blade-battery/); [EEPower](https://eepower.com/tech-insights/byd-launches-faster-charging-2nd-gen-blade-battery/); [Zecar](https://zecar.com/reviews/byd-blade-battery-2-flash-charging-2026)
- BYD's Super e-Platform (March 2025) uses a 1000V, 1000A architecture with a 1MW peak and claims about 400km of range in five minutes. Later charger hardware is quoted up to 1500kW per gun — [BYD](https://www.byd.com/mea/news-list/byd-unveils-super-e-platform-with-megawatt-flash-charging); [BYD media](https://media.byd.com/la-super-e-platform-de-byd-ouvre-lere-du-carburant-et-de-lelectricite-a-la-meme-vitesse-grace-a-la-recharge-eclair-par-megawatts-384237/?lang=eng); [CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-news/byd-confirms-flash-megawatt-charging-for-australia-in-game-changing-knock-out-punch-to)
- BYD targets 20,000 Flash stations in China by the end of 2026 and 6,000 outside China, starting with Europe from mid-2026 — [Zecar](https://zecar.com/reviews/byd-blade-battery-2-flash-charging-2026)
- Solid-state: BYD and CATL (and Geely) target pilot or small-batch production in 2027, with large-scale commercialisation around 2030. BYD's cells are sulfide-based at about 400Wh/kg. CATL chairman Zeng called large-scale (1 million vehicle) production before 2030 "a very small possibility" — [EVWire](https://evwire.com/p/geely-byd-catl-solid-state-battery-2027-pilot); [Electric Future, Oct 2026](https://electricfuture.org/2026/10/26/china-solid-state-battery-race-2027/); [Tecmambo](https://tecmambo.com/explainers/catl-byd-solid-state-battery-2027-trial-production)
- Sodium-ion: CATL and Changan revealed a sodium-ion passenger car for a mid-2026 market launch. BYD says it has sodium cells with up to 10,000 cycles but has set no mass-production date — [CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/02/09/byd-has-developed-sodium-batteries-cycle-life-10000/); [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/u8THQ54BoicNoOgCGcy9) (lower-confidence source)
- On sale in AU now: Zeekr 7X at about 450kW peak and Xpeng G6 at 451kW, both 800V — [CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-news/top-11-fastest-charging-electric-cars-in-australia-including-2026-zeekr-7x-bmw-ix3-and)

### Inferences
- Solid-state cars won't reach Australian showrooms at mass-market prices in 2027. A 2027 pilot in China means about 1,000 demo cars, so waiting for solid-state means waiting until about 2030 or later.
- Sodium-ion targets cheap small cars, not powerful SUVs, so it's irrelevant to this brief.
- 800V with 400kW+ peak is already available under $90k (Zeekr 7X, Xpeng G6). The next step (1000V, 1MW) is real but tied to BYD/Denza and a proprietary network.

- I didn't find CATL Shenxing Plus/Pro specs or AU models using them.
- I didn't verify the MG4 semi-solid-state AU status, or Nio/IM semi-solid packs.
- The search didn't confirm any Sealion 7 or Atto 3 update with Blade 2.0 or Flash charging for AU.

## 3. Can Australian chargers deliver 350kW+ or megawatt speeds?

### Takeaway
Only a handful of public sites exceed 350kW. The best are 400kW ABB units (Ampol Eastern Creek NSW, plus one site in WA and one on the Hume). Megawatt charging will exist only at a few Denza dealers (Sydney, Melbourne, Adelaide) from late 2026 or early 2027. For most Australian road trips, 2027 charging will still be capped around 350 to 400kW, and usually lower in practice.

### Cited Findings
- Ampol (AmpCharge) Eastern Creek, Sydney's M4: two ABB 400kW units opened January 2026, plus eight 360kW posts. They're joint-fastest nationally alongside a 400kW WA site and one on the Hume south of Sydney — [electrive](https://www.electrive.com/2026/02/02/abb-and-ampol-roll-out-first-400-kw-ultra-fast-chargers-in-australia/); [Drive](https://www.drive.com.au/news/australias-fastest-electric-car-chargers-have-officially-opened/); [EV Central](https://evcentral.com.au/leading-the-charge-sydney-scores-two-new-400kw-ultra-fast-dc-chargers-as-ampol-boosts-ev-charging-infrastructure/)

## 4. Model-year update cadence and depreciation

### Takeaway
Chinese brands refresh fast: the Zeekr 7X got a 2027 update about a year after its AU launch, with more power at the same price. Combined with repeated price cuts, this drives steep depreciation, around 50% after two years for some Chinese EVs. That argues against overpaying now, but it also means any car bought in 2027 will be superseded just as fast.

### Cited Findings
- The Zeekr 7X launched in AU in early 2026. A 2027 update with +25kW at unchanged prices opened for orders on 1 November 2026 — [Zecar](https://zecar.com/reviews/2027-zeekr-7x-update-australia-more-power-same-price); [cars24](https://www.cars24.com.au/car-news/refreshed-2027-zeekr-7x-price-specs-australia/)
- One-year-old EVs lose about 25% of their value versus about 11.5% for petrol cars, and Chinese-brand price cuts push used values down — [cars24](https://www.cars24.com.au/car-advice/resale-value-of-evs/)
- Pickles/Datium data shows the MG4 retained about 50% after two years and the BYD Atto 3 about 54% — [The Nightly](https://thenightly.com.au/australia/why-evs-have-really-poor-resale-value-in-australia-tesla-byd-and-mg-prices-are-falling-faster-than-rivals-c-20112843)
- Carma: Chinese-brand cars priced $20k to $35k fell 11.1% year-on-year versus 8.7% for comparable non-Chinese cars — [Savings.com.au](https://www.savings.com.au/news/used-car-prices-fall-as-chinese-brands-and-evs-lose-value-faster)
- The dealer body AADA warns rapid brand expansion exposes owners to faster depreciation, parts shortages and orphaned vehicles — [EV Central](https://evcentral.com.au/stop-now-dealers-demand-new-brand-freeze-as-chinese-ev-wave-heads-for-australia/)
- A single secondary source claims used-EV values have been stabilising since January 2026 and rising since March 2026 on fuel prices (low confidence) — [Gridly](https://www.gridly.com.au/blog/ev-depreciation-australia/)

### Inferences
- Expect any current Chinese SUV to get a mid-cycle update within 12 to 18 months of AU launch, often with more power, range or features at the same price. That makes the run-out stock of the outgoing model the best value buy.
- Leasing or a novated lease limits depreciation risk.

### Gaps
- There's no systematic data on AU update cadence by brand (BYD, Xpeng, Leapmotor and others).
- There's no residual-value data for 2025/26 premium Chinese SUVs (Zeekr, Denza, Xpeng).

## 5. Expert opinions: buy now or wait?

### Takeaway
Available commentary leans towards buying now if a current model meets your needs. Wait only for a specific model that's confirmed and arriving within about three to six months and that clearly beats what's on sale. Don't wait for "coming soon" announcements, megawatt charging or solid-state.

### Cited Findings
- CHOICE: with prices falling and many new models, 2026 "could potentially be a pretty good year to jump on board" — [CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/transport/cars/eco-friendly/articles/is-now-a-good-time-to-buy-an-electric-vehicle-in-australia)
- EV Financer: wait only if a fitting model arrives within a few months. Waiting for its own sake gives up running-cost savings and time-limited incentives — [EV Financer](https://evfinancer.com.au/blog/new-evs-coming-to-australia)
- Drive Electric calls 2026 a strong year to buy. Wait only for a confirmed model due within three to six months, and avoid waiting on announcements without delivery dates — [Drive Electric](https://www.drive-electric.com.au/post/is-now-a-good-time-to-buy-an-ev-in-australia-or-should-you-wait)
- Launches slip: the new Nissan Leaf was postponed indefinitely for AU, Kia said "don't hold your breath" on the EV2, and the Denza Z9 GT slipped to 2027 — [Gridly](https://www.gridly.com.au/blog/upcoming-electric-cars-australia-2027/); [CarExpert via search](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/denza-z9-gt-delayed-electric-shooting-brake-launch-pushed-to-2027)
- Zeekr's own 8X/9X timing and order were described as not final — [WhichCar](https://www.whichcar.com.au/news/zeekr-8x-9x-large-suvs-confirmed-australia)

### Inferences (decision framing for the writer)
- **Buy now** if you want a fast five-seat EV SUV: the 2027-spec Zeekr 7X (500kW, about 450kW charging, $72,900 BOC) is already the updated model and arrives from December 2026. Alternatively, negotiate hard on run-out 2026 stock.
- **Wait about three to six months** if you need a three-row or long-range EREV SUV under $90k. G9L pricing is due by year-end 2026 and the Avatr 07L lands in H1 2027. Waiting a few weeks for G9L pricing costs little.
- **Don't wait** for megawatt charging, solid-state or sodium-ion. They're confined to a few Denza sites, sit in luxury models above $90k, or are years away.
- The Zeekr 8X is the main wildcard for a "powerful PHEV under $90k" buyer, but its pricing is only an estimate (about $85k) and it may arrive mid-to-late 2027.

### Gaps
- I didn't find a Drive, CarExpert, The Driven or EV Council article directly addressing buy-now-vs-wait in late 2026. The opinions above come from CHOICE and EV-finance or retail blogs, which are lower authority.
