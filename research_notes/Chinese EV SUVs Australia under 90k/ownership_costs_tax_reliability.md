# Ownership costs, tax, reliability, support and safety: Chinese EV/PHEV SUVs in Australia (and NZ) under ~AUD $90k, as of November 2026

Research method note: web search worked, but direct page fetches from carexpert.com.au, mynrma.com.au and ato.gov.au were blocked by this session's network policy. Most findings below therefore come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Figures that matter for a purchase decision (LCT threshold, FBT dates, warranty terms) should be checked against the primary page before the buyer commits.

## 1. Tax and incentives (FBT, LCT, stamp duty, NZ RUC)

### Takeaway
The EV FBT exemption still applies in full in November 2026 for EVs under the LCT threshold. A Budget 2026 change (announced 5 May 2026) keeps it whole until 31 March 2027. From 1 April 2027 the full exemption is capped at $75,000, and EVs priced above $75k but under the LCT threshold get only a 25% discount. From 1 April 2029 a permanent 25% discount replaces the full exemption. Commitments already in place are reported to be unaffected. New PHEVs lost the FBT exemption from 1 April 2025. The 2026-27 fuel-efficient LCT threshold is $91,661, so a ~$90k buyer is just under it. The "fuel-efficient" test is now ≤3.5 L/100km.

### Cited Findings
**FBT electric car discount (novated lease/salary packaging)**
- The government announced phased changes on 5 May 2026, ahead of the 12 May 2026-27 Budget. The full exemption continues until the end of March 2027. From 1 April 2027 the full exemption applies only to eligible EVs valued at $75,000 or less. EVs above $75k but under the LCT threshold get a 25% discount. ### Inferences
- For a ~$90k budget, the price paid matters for tax. Under $91,661 for a qualifying EV or ≤3.5 L/100km PHEV avoids LCT, which is 33% on the amount over the threshold (the rate is from my training knowledge, not verified this session). Most Chinese PHEVs advertise combined figures under 3.5 L/100km, so they likely use the higher threshold. A conventional hybrid or petrol SUV over $80,809 pays LCT.
- The FBT timing creates a deadline. An EV novated lease committed before 1 April 2027 gets the full exemption whatever its price, if under the LCT threshold. After that date, an $80–90k EV gets only a 25% discount, while one under $75k keeps the full exemption. This should push novated buyers toward sub-$75k EVs, or toward committing before April 2027.
- PHEVs bought new now get no FBT benefit, which weakens the novated-lease case for PHEVs relative to BEVs.

### Gaps
- I could not open the ATO pages directly to confirm the exact 2026-27 thresholds, nor whether the April 2027 FBT changes have passed Parliament.
- Whether the $75k cap is measured on the LCT value or the base value is not confirmed.
- State duty positions for Vic, Qld and ACT conflict across secondary sources. The state revenue offices should be checked.
- NZ Clean Car Discount and Clean Car Standard changes after 2023 were not researched this session. My training knowledge is that the Clean Car Discount ended 31 Dec 2023, but this was not verified here.

## 2. NVES effect on pricing

### Takeaway
The New Vehicle Efficiency Standard started 1 January 2025, and penalties and credits accrue from 1 July 2025. Chinese EV/PHEV-heavy brands such as BYD, GWM and Chery are expected to be credit generators. Compliance costs are expected to push up prices of high-emitting models from legacy brands. No official price-impact data was found.

### Cited Findings
- NVES took effect 1 Jan 2025, with emissions tracking for penalties and credits from 1 July 2025. Eight of 2025's top-10 sellers face penalties in 2026. Low emitters (Tesla, Polestar, BYD) can sell credits, and BYD, GWM and Chery are expected to benefit. — [carsales](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/how-nves-will-change-the-new-car-market-151253)
- GoAuto, a commentary piece, estimates Chinese makers' credit position could be worth roughly A$13k–17k of price advantage. It also says major legacy brands were non-compliant in H2 2025. This is a single, unverified estimate. — [GoAuto premium](https://premium.goauto.com.au/huge-nves-gift-from-canberra/)

### Inferences
- NVES likely supports continued aggressive Chinese EV/PHEV pricing, since credits subsidise their low-emission models. That in turn adds depreciation pressure on used Chinese EVs (see section 3).

### Gaps
- No regulator (DCCEEW/NVES regulator) data on credit prices or measured pass-through to retail prices.

## 3. Depreciation and resale

### Takeaway
EVs in Australia have depreciated steeply, with Tesla, BYD and MG among the worst affected, and new-car price cuts are a major driver. There is little brand-specific Redbook/Glass's data for BYD, Zeekr or Xpeng in public sources. Chinese PHEVs and newer brands have too little used-market history to judge.

### Cited Findings
- Savvy's analysis of RedBook data found a 2022 Tesla Model Y RWD about 48% below its new price, and the new-price cuts are cited as a cause. Used EV prices are down "as much as 48 per cent". — [EV Central](https://evcentral.com.au/its-a-buyers-market-used-ev-prices-plunge-as-more-models-arrive/); [Savvy](https://savvy.com.au/car-loans/car-depreciation-comparison/); [Paint & Panel](https://www.paintandpanel.com.au/news/news/used-ev-prices-plunging-as-much-as-48-per-cent-creating-a-buyer-s-market)
- The Nightly reports Tesla, BYD and MG prices falling faster than rivals, and says the MG4 retained only about 50% of its value. The article date is unconfirmed. — [The Nightly](https://thenightly.com.au/australia/why-evs-have-really-poor-resale-value-in-australia-tesla-byd-and-mg-prices-are-falling-faster-than-rivals-c-20112843)
- BYD Australia's COO says each price cut is weighed against its impact on existing owners' resale. — [PortNews/ACM](https://www.portnews.com.au/story/9361914/byd-denies-its-starting-a-price-war-in-oz/)
- CarExpert covered a BYD importer putting EV depreciation "in the spotlight". — [CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/byd-importer-puts-electric-car-depreciation-in-the-spotlight)
- RedBook's June 2026 three-year residual forecasts are topped by petrol/hybrid Toyotas (Corolla and Corolla Cross, 80–81%). No Chinese brand appears in the top list. — [Canstar](https://www.canstar.com.au/car-insurance/car-depreciation-rates/)
- Insurance Business reports EV appetite rising while "confidence in resale value collapses". — [Insurance Business AU](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/auto-motor/ev-appetite-rises-as-confidence-in-resale-value-collapses-588328.aspx)
- A Canadian guide puts BYD three-year retention at 50–60% in Australia/UK. Its methodology is unclear, so treat it with caution. — [ThinkEV](https://thinkev.ca/blog/chinese-ev-resale-value-canada-depreciation-guide-2026)

### Inferences
- Expect roughly 45–55% value loss over three years for Chinese BEVs bought at full RRP. This range is inferred from the Model Y and MG4 data points, not a sourced BYD figure. Frequent new-price cuts and NVES-subsidised pricing compound this. Buying at a run-out or discounted price, or leasing via novated (where residual risk sits with the driver at balloon), changes the calculus.
- Brands with distributor upheaval (Xpeng) or small networks (Zeekr) likely face extra resale discounting. This is an inference only.

### Gaps
- No public Redbook/Glass's brand-level retention figures were found for BYD, GWM, Chery, Zeekr, Xpeng, Leapmotor or Deepal.
- No data was found on PHEV resale versus BEV resale for Chinese brands.

## 8. Running costs (charging, insurance, tyres)

### Takeaway
Home charging costs about $4–7 per 100 km, against about $11–25 for petrol. EV insurance runs about 10–40% higher than for petrol cars, and premiums rose about 10% in the year to March 2026. No Chinese-brand-specific AU insurance data was found.

### Cited Findings

## 9. Safety (ANCAP) and ADAS annoyance

### Takeaway
Most mainstream Chinese SUVs in the segment hold five-star ANCAP ratings: BYD Sealion 7, Zeekr 7X, Xpeng G6, Leapmotor C10 and B10. ADAS intrusiveness (lane-keep tugging, speed and driver-monitor beeps) is a recurring reviewer complaint, notably for the Jaecoo J7, and Chery has admitted it needs to improve.
