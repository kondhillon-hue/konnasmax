# Zeekr's 500kW 7X leads China's power-SUV charge

The best Chinese-brand SUV under about AUD $90,000 drive-away for a buyer who puts big power first is the **2027 Zeekr 7X Performance AWD**. It makes **500kW/790Nm**, costs **$72,900 before on-roads (roughly $77–80k drive-away)**, charges at up to about **420–450kW on an 800V system**, has air suspension and a **five-star ANCAP rating**, and has been in showrooms since November 2026 with first deliveries in December. If raw kilowatts are all that matters, the **MG IM6 Performance (572kW, 3.4s, about $78–81k drive-away)** has the most power in the bracket and the longest warranty (10 years/250,000km). It is let down by poor efficiency and unconfirmed charging and safety data. Below those two, the **BYD Sealion 7 Performance (390kW, about $64k)** and **Xpeng G6 AWD Performance (358kW, $63.8k)** give the most power and charging speed per dollar. Among plug-in hybrids, the **BYD Sealion 8 Premium AWD (359kW, seven seats)**, **GWM Haval H6 GT Hi4 (321kW, about $53k drive-away)** and **GWM Tank 500 Hi4-T (300kW/750Nm, 3,000kg towing)** lead. Timing matters as much as the model. The EV fringe benefits tax (FBT) exemption narrows on **1 April 2027**, new PHEVs already get no FBT exemption, and no technology due in 2027 (megawatt charging, solid-state batteries) will matter on Australian roads. For most buyers that makes **"buy now, or by March 2027"** the right answer. The exception is a three-row family buyer, who should wait a few weeks for Xpeng G9L pricing. One caution runs through everything: most prices here come from search-result summaries of Australian motoring sites, not from reading the pages in full, and many are promotional. Confirm every figure with a dealer quote.

### Why the 7X edges the IM6 despite 72 fewer kilowatts

The 2027 Zeekr 7X update adds **25kW to every variant at unchanged prices**, which lifts the Performance AWD to **500kW/790Nm** ([Zecar](https://zecar.com/reviews/2027-zeekr-7x-update-australia-more-power-same-price); [The Beep](https://thebeep.com.au/car-news/2027-zeekr-7x-update-price-specs-australia/)). That puts it within 72kW of the IM6's **572kW/802Nm**, while the 7X is clearly ahead on almost everything else. CarExpert measured the 2026 7X Performance at **18.9kWh/100km** ([CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/2026-zeekr-7x-review)) against **24.1kWh/100km for the IM6** ([CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/2025-im6-review)). With similar 100kWh packs, that works out to roughly 500km of real-world range for the Zeekr and about 400km for the MG (my estimate, assuming close to 100kWh usable). The 7X is the only car in the group confirmed with **air suspension and double-wishbone/multi-link hardware**. EV Central says that hardware is what the roughly $9k premium over the Long Range RWD really buys ([EV Central](https://evcentral.com.au/2026-zeekr-7x-long-range-rwd-review-is-this-actually-the-7x-to-buy/)). Chasing Cars praised its "tangible luxury, terrific build quality and cutting-edge technology for a surprisingly sharp price" ([Chasing Cars](https://www.chasingcars.com.au/reviews/midsize-suvs/zeekr-7x-performance-awd-2026-review/)). It also scored **five ANCAP stars** with 91% adult and 87% child occupant protection ([CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-news/top-marks-for-chinese-newcomers-2026-leapmotor-b10-and-zeekr-7x-among-electrified-cars-to)).

The IM6 Performance's case is simple and strong. It is the quickest SUV here (**3.4s**), carsales says its "pricing, equipment and performance bang for buck is huge" ([carsales](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/im-presented-by-mg-im6-performance-2026-review-151767/)), and MG backs it with **10 years/250,000km** of warranty cover ([carsales business](https://business.carsales.com.au/news-room/mg-motor-australia-announces-market-leading-10-year-warranty/)). The research could not confirm its charging rate, voltage or ANCAP rating. For a buyer who road-trips, those gaps matter more than an extra 0.4 seconds to 100km/h. Pure power seekers should still put it first.

### The value tier gives up charging speed or polish, not power

The **Sealion 7 Performance** delivers 390kW (160kW front, 230kW rear) and 4.5s for about $64k before on-roads ([CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/2026-byd-sealion-7-performance-review)). Sources disagree on whether $63,990 is a drive-away or before-on-roads figure, and carsales puts indicative drive-away at $66,215–70,442 ([carsales](https://www.carsales.com.au/research/byd/sealion-7/)). The compromise is charging. Its LFP pack peaks at **150kW DC** ([Aussiemotor](https://aussiemotor.com.au/blog/byd-sealion-7-premium-vs-performance-australia-2026-is-the-9000-upgrade-worth-it)), about a third of what the 800V rivals can take, and WLTP range is a modest 456km. In return the Sealion 7 holds a five-star ANCAP rating with 87% adult and 93% child occupant protection ([ANCAP](https://www.ancap.com.au/media-and-gallery/media-releases/a478ae)) and comes with the largest single-brand EV network in the country.

The **Xpeng G6 AWD Performance** is the opposite trade. It has less power (358kW, 4.1s) but **800V charging at a reported 451kW peak** and a claimed 12-minute 10–80% charge for $63,800 before on-roads ([Carsauce](https://carsauce.com/news/2026-xpeng-g6-australia-price-specs-orders-open); [CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-news/top-11-fastest-charging-electric-cars-in-australia-including-2026-zeekr-7x-bmw-ix3-and)). The facelift cut prices by $3k and comes with a **10-year warranty** ([CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-news/new-chinese-brand-returns-fire-with-10-year-warranty-on-its-2025-xpeng-g6-as-battle-with)). The risk is the brand rather than the car: Xpeng dropped its distributor TrueEV, which is now suing (more under dealer networks below).

The **Smart #5 Brabus** is a Zeekr 7X twin: 475kW, a 100kWh pack (94kWh usable), 400kW DC charging and 540km WLTP ([Drive](https://www.drive.com.au/news/2027-smart-5-price-and-specs-zeekr-7x-twin-detailed-for-australia/)). It costs **$1,000 more than a 7X Performance**, has the pre-update 475kW tune, and keeps the RWD's brakes and suspension, so it has no air suspension. It ranks fourth because it lost the power race to its own sibling. It suits buyers who prefer its styling or live near one of the LSH Auto dealers in Melbourne, Sydney and Brisbane, where deliveries began in November 2026.

### Plug-in hybrids win on towing and seats, not speed

The fastest PHEVs top out below the 400kW BEVs, and their headline figures need scrutiny. The **Omoda 9** claims 395kW, but only in Sport mode at 90% charge or more, and EV Central's power meter peaked at about **230–240kW** ([EV Central](https://evcentral.com.au/2025-omoda-9-review-new-premium-plug-in-hybrid-takes-the-fight-to-tesla-byd-zeekr-mazda-volvo-and-more/)). It misses the top eight for that reason. The **Sealion 8 Premium AWD** is the verified PHEV power leader at **359kW and 4.9s**. It adds seven seats, a 21-speaker DiSound system, a 360-degree camera, 2,000kg towing and 74kW DC charging ([Exhaust Notes](https://www.exhaustnotes.com.au/2026-byd-sealion-8-premium-awd-car-review/); [EV Central](https://evcentral.com.au/cars/byd/sealion-8/)). It had **no ANCAP or Euro NCAP rating** at the time of reporting.

The **Haval H6 GT Hi4** offers **321kW and 4.6s for $52,990 drive-away**, the best power per dollar of any plug-in hybrid. Its 183km NEDC electric range is a class-leading claim ([TheBeep](https://thebeep.com.au/car-news/2026-gwm-haval-h6-gt-phev-price-range-australia/)), but RACQ criticised its "poorly calibrated ADAS" ([RACQ](https://www.racq.com.au/articles/car-reviews/gwm-haval-h6gt-phev)). The **Tank 500 Hi4-T** pairs a 2.0-litre turbo with a 120kW motor for **300kW/750Nm, 3,000kg braked towing and 6kW V2L** ([RV Daily](https://rvdaily.com.au/gwm-tank-500-hi4-t-phev-caravan-towing/)) at $79,990 before on-roads ([WhichCar](https://www.whichcar.com.au/news/gwm-pricing-specs-tank-500-plug-in-hybrid-aussie-expansion-plans)). No drive-away price was found, and it may land close to the $90k ceiling. Two strong alternatives missed the cut: the **Chery Tiggo 9 CSH Ultimate** and **Jaecoo J8 SHS Summit**, both seven-seaters with 315kW/580Nm at $59,990 before on-roads. CarExpert rates both "Very Good" ([CarExpert Tiggo 9](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/2026-chery-tiggo-9-review); [CarExpert J8](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/2026-jaecoo-j8-shs-review)). They suit buyers who want a seven-seat PHEV for less money than the Sealion 8 and can accept 44kW less power.

The **Polestar 4 Long Range Dual Motor** (400kW, 94kWh, 590km WLTP, China-built under a Geely-owned brand) sits just outside the list. Its $88,350 before-on-roads price ([Polestar media](https://media.polestar.com/releases/796?lang=en_AU)) almost certainly exceeds $90k once on-road costs are added, and any option pack pushes it further: CarsGuide lists the Plus Pack version at $97,839 ([CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/polestar/4/price/2026/long-range-dual-motor-pack)).

## 800V NMC packs charge three times faster than 400V LFP

Charging architecture separates the field more sharply than power does. The 800V cars, the **Zeekr 7X (about 420–450kW peak; 10–80% claimed in as little as 11 minutes), Xpeng G6 (451kW) and Smart #5 (400kW, under 18 minutes)**, charge at three times the rate of the Sealion 7's 150kW ([CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/2026-zeekr-7x-price-and-specs); [CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-news/top-11-fastest-charging-electric-cars-in-australia-including-2026-zeekr-7x-bmw-ix3-and)). The Australian 7X uses an **800V** system, not the newer 900V Chinese-market setup, with a 100kWh NMC pack ([EV Central](https://evcentral.com.au/cars/zeekr/7x/)). Chemistry brings its own trade-offs. BYD's Blade LFP packs give up charge rate and energy density for durability and lower cost, while NMC packs (Zeekr) support the faster peaks. BYD backs its LFP with an 8-year/160,000km battery warranty that guarantees at least **70% capacity retention** ([BYD warranty PDF](https://bydautomotive.com.au/public/vehicle-warranty-2026.pdf)).

These peak figures outrun Australia's chargers. The fastest public units are **400kW ABB chargers** at Ampol Eastern Creek on Sydney's M4, at one site in WA and at one on the Hume ([electrive](https://www.electrive.com/2026/02/02/abb-and-ampol-roll-out-first-400-kw-ultra-fast-chargers-in-australia/)). Even these fall short of what the 7X can take at its best ([CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-news/top-11-fastest-charging-electric-cars-in-australia-including-2026-zeekr-7x-bmw-ix3-and)). In practice, any 800V car here already charges as fast as almost every Australian public charger allows. Its real advantage over a 150kW LFP car is a 10–80% stop of about 15 minutes against roughly half an hour.

PHEV charging is slower still. DC peaks are **74kW (Sealion 8 AWD), 71kW (Tiggo 9, international spec), 70kW (J8) and 48kW (H6 GT)**, and the **MG HS Super Hybrid has no DC charging at all** ([EV Central](https://evcentral.com.au/2025-mg-hs-super-hybrid-essence-long-term-review-4-a-bunch-of-things-we-love-and-dont-about-mgs-plug-in-medium-suv/)). The 34–37kWh packs in high-power PHEVs give roughly **100–150km of WLTP electric range**, and about 120–130km in real use for the Chery-group cars. With a flat battery, consumption climbs steeply. CarExpert recorded **6.3L/100km** in a depleted Tiggo 9 against an official 1.3L/100km ([CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/2026-chery-tiggo-9-review)). A PHEV only delivers its low running costs if it is plugged in most nights.

## Warranty and dealer depth split the field more than safety ratings

Every BEV in the top five with a published ANCAP result has five stars: **Zeekr 7X (2026), Sealion 7 (2025) and Xpeng G6 (2024)** ([The Driven](https://thedriven.io/2024/12/06/new-chinese-ev-models-from-leapmotor-xpeng-and-zeekr-obtain-5-star-ancap-safety-rating/amp/)). ANCAP moved to new protocols in 2026, so newer results are not directly comparable with older ones. The PHEVs are weaker on verified safety. The **Sealion 8 and J8 SHS were unrated**, the J8's third row has no airbag coverage ([CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/2026-jaecoo-j8-shs-review)), and ratings for the H6 GT PHEV, Tanks, Tiggo 9 and Omoda 9 could not be verified. Overactive driver-assist systems are the most common complaint across the segment, and Chery has admitted it has "still got a lot of work to do" on active safety ([CarsGuide](https://carsguide.com.au/car-news/weve-still-got-a-lot-of-work-to-do-chery-admits-it-needs-to-do-better-on-active-safety%C2%A0as)).

Warranty terms vary more. **MG leads at 10 years/250,000km**. **GWM's H6 GT has 7 years/unlimited km plus 8 years/unlimited km on the battery** ([RACQ](https://www.racq.com.au/articles/car-reviews/gwm-haval-h6gt-phev)). BYD's vehicle cover is a comparatively short **6 years/150,000km** ([BYD warranty PDF](https://bydautomotive.com.au/public/vehicle-warranty-2026.pdf)), and **Zeekr offers 5 years/unlimited km plus 8 years/160,000km on the battery** ([CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/2026-zeekr-7x-price-and-specs)). Dealer counts matter just as much in regional Australia. December 2025 data shows **GWM with 120 dealers, MG 115, BYD 91 (targeting 150) and Chery 86, but Zeekr only 12** ([CarExpert on BDO](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/chinese-brands-claim-a-quarter-of-aussie-auto-market-but-accounting-firm-says-boom-wont-last)). Xpeng is going factory-direct after splitting from TrueEV, which is suing. It plans **66 Australian and 7 NZ locations by December 2026** and has pledged to honour TrueEV-era warranties ([The Driven](https://thedriven.io/2026/10/27/xpeng-expands-dealer-and-service-network-across-australia-and-new-zealand-ahead-of-new-electric-models/); [CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/xpeng-australia-pledges-to-honour-axed-distributors-promises)). The thin network is the 7X's main weakness. A buyer outside a capital city should check the distance to the nearest Zeekr service point before ordering, or choose the MG or BYD.

## FBT's 1 April 2027 deadline matters more than any spec

Tax shapes the decision more than any spec sheet. The **EV FBT exemption still applies in full** until 31 March 2027. From **1 April 2027**, the full exemption covers only EVs valued at **$75,000 or less**, and EVs between $75k and the LCT threshold get just a **25% discount**. From 1 April 2029, new arrangements get only a permanent 25% discount ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com.au/tax/tax-alerts/government-announces-phased-changes-to-the-FBT-electric-car-exemption.html); [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/new-legislation/in-detail/businesses/electric-car-discount-more-sustainable-fbt-treatment-of-electric-cars)). Existing commitments are reported as unaffected, but sources disagree on whether the change has passed Parliament. Several leading cars sit right at the $75k line: the Zeekr 7X Performance ($72,900 before on-roads), Smart #5 Brabus ($73,900) and IM6 Performance (about $78–81k drive-away). The research did not confirm whether the cap is measured on the LCT value or another base. A novated-lease buyer who wants one of these cars should **commit before 1 April 2027** to lock in the full exemption. One industry estimate puts the saving at about **$4,700 a year on a $50,000 EV** ([Savvy](https://savvy.com.au/novated-leasing/fbt-exemption-changes-2026/)), and it grows with the car's price.

**New PHEVs have had no FBT exemption since 1 April 2025**. Only arrangements committed before that date keep it, and any change to the arrangement ends it ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/fbtphev)). That removes the main salary-packaging argument for the Sealion 8, H6 GT or Tank 500. The **2026–27 luxury car tax (LCT) threshold is $91,661 for fuel-efficient vehicles and $80,809 for others** ([ABF Customs Notice 2026-19](https://www.abf.gov.au/help-and-support-subsite/CustomsNotices/2026-19.pdf)). The fuel-efficient test tightened from 7L/100km to **3.5L/100km** ([Chasing Cars](https://www.chasingcars.com.au/news/financing/changes-to-luxury-car-tax-for-2026-will-hit-toyota-kluger-mazda-cx-90-audi-q5-and-australian-family-suv-buyers-hardest/)). Every BEV here sits below the higher threshold. The Tank 500 is the one to check: at $79,990 before on-roads it is close to the $80,809 standard threshold, so its official combined fuel figure decides which threshold applies. That figure was not found. In New Zealand, road user charges are **NZ$76 per 1,000km for BEVs and NZ$38 for PHEVs** ([Calculate.co.nz](https://www.calculate.co.nz/reference/nz-road-user-charges.php)). The 7X Performance lists at **NZ$94,990** before on-roads ([EVs and Beyond](https://evsandbeyond.co.nz/zeekr-7x-confirmed-for-new-zealand-from-69990/)), and the Sealion 8 Premium AWD at NZ$92,990, with an offer of NZ$89,990 ([NZ Autocar](https://nzautocar.co.nz/vehicles/sealion-8-premium-awd/)).

Running costs favour the BEVs, but depreciation eats much of that saving. Home charging costs about **$5–7 per 100km against $12–15 for petrol** ([Solar Choice](https://www.solarchoice.net.au/ev/charging/cost-to-charge-ev-in-australia)). At 30c/kWh, that works out to about $5.70/100km for the 7X and $7.20 for the thirstier IM6 (my calculation from CarExpert's test figures). EV insurance runs **0–40% above comparable petrol cars** ([Taylor Fry](https://taylorfry.com.au/articles/australias-ev-boom-how-insurers-need-to-respond/)). The largest cost is resale. One-year-old EVs lose about **25% of their value against 11.5% for petrol cars** ([cars24](https://www.cars24.com.au/car-advice/resale-value-of-evs/)), and Pickles/Datium data shows the MG4 kept about **50% after two years** and the Atto 3 about 54% ([The Nightly](https://thenightly.com.au/australia/why-evs-have-really-poor-resale-value-in-australia-tesla-byd-and-mg-prices-are-falling-faster-than-rivals-c-20112843)). There is no public resale data for Zeekr, Xpeng or the Chinese PHEVs. Repeated new-car price cuts drive this depreciation, and the New Vehicle Efficiency Standard (NVES) gives low-emission brands credits that let them keep cutting ([carsales](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/how-nves-will-change-the-new-car-market-151253)). A buyer can limit the damage by paying run-out or discounted prices, or by leasing.

## 2027 brings big PHEVs and faster charging, but no game-changer under $90k

The 2027 pipeline is busy, but few new arrivals have confirmed pricing under $90k. The **Xpeng G9L**, a three-row EREV on the 800V platform with 1,200km combined range, is due in showrooms in early 2027 with pricing promised by the end of 2026. Drive reports a target of about **$75,000–90,000** ([Drive](https://www.drive.com.au/news/2027-xpeng-g9l-seven-seater-coming-to-australia-next-year/)). The **Avatr 07L** (800V silicon-carbide platform, CATL cells) opens pre-orders before the end of 2026 for first-half 2027 deliveries. Projected pricing is about $70–75k before on-roads ([carsales](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/avatr-eyes-higher-prices-than-zeekr-in-australia-153494/)). The **Zeekr 8X**, a range-extender PHEV headlined at **1,030kW**, is confirmed for 2027, and one outlet estimates its price at about $85,000 ([CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-news/zeekrs-new-1030kw-suv-confirmed-zeekr-8x-to-arrive-in-2027-as-hyped-new-luxury-plug-in)). Zeekr's own CEO says the 8X and 9X launch order is not final ([WhichCar](https://www.whichcar.com.au/news/zeekr-8x-9x-large-suvs-confirmed-australia)). The Xpeng G7, HQ (Hongqi) E-HS9, Chery's iCaur and the Jetour entry are confirmed in some form. Xiaomi is not expected before 2028 at the earliest ([CarNewsChina](https://carnewschina.com/2026/11/05/xiaomi-sets-2028-global-right-hand-drive-push-as-australia-hiring-begins/)).

Battery and charging technology is moving fast in China, but little of it will reach Australian roads by 2027. BYD's second-generation Blade pack claims **10–70% in five minutes** on its 1,000V megawatt "Flash" system ([Battery-News](https://battery-news.de/en/2026/03/06/byd-unveils-second-generation-of-blade-battery/)). In Australia, though, the only Flash sites planned are **three Denza showrooms** in Sydney, Melbourne and Adelaide, and the only confirmed local model, the Denza Z9 GT, is a wagon that has slipped to 2027 and is expected to cost well above $90k ([electrive](https://www.electrive.com/2026/05/04/byd-to-establish-flash-charging-stations-in-australia-and-new-zealand/); [Drive](https://www.drive.com.au/news/denza-z9-gt-hits-uk-with-200000-price-but-the-blow-may-be-softer-in-australia/)). BYD, CATL and Geely all target **pilot solid-state production in 2027 and mass rollout around 2030**. CATL's chairman called large-scale output before 2030 "a very small possibility" ([Electric Future](https://electricfuture.org/2026/10/26/china-solid-state-battery-race-2027/)). Sodium-ion is aimed at cheap small cars, not 400kW SUVs.

## Buy now, unless you need three rows

Expert opinion converges on one rule: buy now if a current model fits your needs, and wait only for a *confirmed* model arriving within three to six months that clearly beats what is on sale ([Drive Electric](https://www.drive-electric.com.au/post/is-now-a-good-time-to-buy-an-ev-in-australia-or-should-you-wait); [CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/transport/cars/eco-friendly/articles/is-now-a-good-time-to-buy-an-electric-vehicle-in-australia)). Launch dates slip often. The Z9 GT slipped to 2027, and Nissan's new Leaf was postponed indefinitely ([Gridly](https://www.gridly.com.au/blog/upcoming-electric-cars-australia-2027/)). Applied to this brief, the advice splits by buyer. **Five-seat BEV buyers should buy now.** The 2027 7X *is* the update, with orders open since 1 November and deliveries from December, so there is no newer version to wait for. Anyone offered run-out 2026 stock at 475kW should demand a large discount, because the 500kW car costs the same. **Novated-lease buyers should buy before 1 April 2027**, because for cars priced near $75k that date is worth more than any 2027 spec change. **Seven-seat and EREV buyers should wait a few weeks to a few months.** G9L pricing is due by year-end and the Avatr 07L lands in the first half of 2027, while the Sealion 8 and Tiggo 9 will still be there and are increasingly discounted. **Nobody should wait for megawatt charging, solid-state or sodium-ion**, because none of them arrives in an Australian SUV under $90k in 2027. The 1,030kW Zeekr 8X is the only real wildcard for a power-first PHEV buyer, and its roughly $85k price is a media estimate, not a confirmed figure.

A note on the evidence. Direct page fetches from CarExpert, Drive, MyNRMA and the ATO were blocked during the research, so most prices, specifications and tax details here come from **search-result summaries** of those and other outlets, not from full-page reads. Many prices are promotions with end dates, for example the Jaecoo J7 offer ends 30 November 2026 and the Sealion 8 carries a $4,000 cut. Sources disagree on several figures, including whether the Sealion 7's $63,990 is drive-away and whether the IM6 costs $77,990 or $80,990. Several items could not be confirmed: the IM6's charging rate and ANCAP rating, the Smart #5's warranty, drive-away prices for the Tank 500 and Tiggo 9, the 2027 7X's 0–100 time, and whether the April 2027 FBT changes have passed Parliament. Get a written drive-away quote for your state, check ancap.com.au and confirm the FBT position with your novated-lease provider before you sign.

## Conclusion

The ceiling on "big power for the money" has moved so quickly that 500kW now costs about $78k drive-away. Buying the most kilowatts is no longer the hard part. The real differences are in what comes with them. Charging architecture (800V NMC against 400V LFP), service-network depth and the FBT treatment on your purchase date now separate these cars more than their 0–100 times do, and those are the questions to put to a dealer. A 72kW gap between the 7X and IM6 changes less in everyday driving than a 300kW gap in charging speed, or the difference between 12 and 115 dealers.

The technology argument for waiting is weaker than it looks, and the tax argument for acting is stronger. Australian public chargers already top out below what current 800V cars can accept, solid-state batteries are about 2030 technology, and each model generation is replaced within 12–18 months. Waiting buys depreciation on the next car rather than escaping it. The deadlines that matter are 1 April 2027 for FBT and each dealer's run-out window. The power-first buyer should pick the car that suits their charging access and their distance from a service centre, then commit to a deal before April.
