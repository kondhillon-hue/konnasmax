# Zeekr 8X out-muscles G9L, Xpeng arrives first

On a power-first brief, the **Zeekr 8X is the better car and the Xpeng G9L is the better buy**. The 8X has between **660kW and 1,030kW** against the G9L range-extender's roughly **370kW**. It also has the richer cabin (Naim audio, AR head-up display, Nappa zero-gravity seats) and slightly faster 900V/6C charging, so it wins a weighted scorecard **7.9 to 7.4**. The G9L wins everything around the car. It seats **seven rather than five**, is targeted at **about A$75,000–90,000**, and should reach showrooms in **early 2027**, about six months before the 8X's **second-half-2027** arrival. It also comes with a **7-year warranty and 66 service points**, against Zeekr's 5 years and roughly 25. **Neither car has Australian pricing yet.** Every AUD figure below is a media estimate or a manufacturer target, and every power, range and charging figure is a China or Europe spec that may change for Australia. Media estimates for the 8X run from **about $80k to $135k**. In China the cheapest 8X costs more than the dearest G9L, so the budget-friendly 8X may turn out to be a lower-output dual-motor grade, or may not exist. Both cars are plug-in hybrids for tax purposes, so **neither gets the FBT novated-lease exemption**. Families should wait a few weeks for G9L pricing, which is due before the end of 2026. Power-first five-seat buyers should note that the **Zeekr 7X Performance (500kW, on sale now, about $77–80k drive-away)**, the earlier report's top pick, beats the G9L on power today and beats the 8X on certainty.

## 660kW against 370kW: the 8X wins the power contest outright

The two cars use different hybrid layouts. The **G9L EREV** drives all four wheels with two electric motors, while a 110kW 1.5-litre turbo engine works purely as a generator. Xpeng quotes **about 370kW (496hp), 695Nm and 0–100km/h in 4.95 seconds** ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/09/17/xpeng-launches-g9l-china/); [EV Powered UK](https://evpowered.co.uk/news/xpeng-g9l-price-and-specifications-confirmed-as-65k-flagship-seven-seater-arrives-in-uk/)). One table quotes 405kW instead ([autocango](https://www.autocango.com/news-detail/xpeng-g9l-launch-vla-distillation)). The G9L BEV AWD makes **430kW and 4.45 seconds**, but it may not come to Australia ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/09/17/xpeng-launches-g9l-china/)). The **8X** pairs a 2.0-litre turbo with either **two motors (660kW/935Nm)** or **three motors (1,030kW/1,410Nm, 0–100km/h in 2.96 seconds)** on Zeekr's 900V SEA-S platform ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeekr_8X); [CarNewsChina](https://carnewschina.com/2026/04/17/zeekr-8x-launched-900v-hybrid-suv-tri-motor-1380-hp-0-100-in-2-96s-starts-45806-usd-in-china/)). Even the dual-motor 8X, the grade most likely to fit a $90k budget, has **about 1.8 times the G9L EREV's power**. The tri-motor has 2.8 times as much, and it is the grade least likely to stay under $90k.

The power difference is not a matter of handling. Reviewers criticise both cars' road manners. What Car? wished the G9L's "handling [were] more secure" ([What Car?](https://www.whatcar.com/xpeng/g9l/suv/review)). Auto Express said the 8X disappoints against European rivals on body control and steering feel ([Auto Express](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/zeekr/369450/new-zeekr-8x-review-1381bhp-plug-hybrid-suv-absolute-tech-fest)), and WhichCar found its weight shows in corners ([WhichCar](https://www.whichcar.com.au/reviews/zeekr-8x-review-tech-rich-plug-in-hybrid-suv-with-massive-power)). The G9L partly offsets this with **rear-wheel steering of up to 15 degrees**, which shrinks its turning circle to 11m ([CarNewsChina](https://carnewschina.com/2026/08/11/xpeng-g9l-revealed-price-with-rear-wheel-steering-755-km-range/); [autohled](https://www.autohled.cz/en/a/xpeng/g9l)). Both are big, heavy SUVs that accelerate hard in a straight line, and the 8X does it much harder.

On electric range they are close. The G9L EREV carries a **63.3kWh LFP pack and a 60L tank**. It is rated at **287km WLTP on electricity and about 1,154–1,200km combined** ([carsales](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/xpeng-g9l-targets-long-range-suv-buyers-153252/); [xpeng.guru](https://xpeng.guru/lineup/xpeng-g9l/)), or 435km and 1,602km on China's optimistic CLTC cycle ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/09/17/xpeng-launches-g9l-china/)). The 8X uses **55.1kWh or 70kWh NMC packs** with **256–328km WLTC electric range** and up to 1,416km combined CLTC on twin-motor versions ([CarNewsChina](https://carnewschina.com/2026/01/08/zeekr-8x-plug-in-hybrid-suv-key-parameters-exposed-275-hp-2-0t-engine-70-kwh-battery-5-1-meters-length/); [CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/04/17/zeekr-launches-8x-suv/)). The bigger difference shows up once the battery is flat. The 8X uses about **6.4–6.5L/100km** on WLTC ([Port Auto](https://www.portauto.com/zeekr-8x-full-specifications-trim-comparison-portauto-export/)), while Xpeng's internal tests put the G9L at **7.8L/100km** in charge-sustaining mode ([xpeng.guru](https://xpeng.guru/lineup/xpeng-g9l/)). The cycles differ, but on these numbers the 8X burns about 17% less fuel on long trips, even though it has the bigger engine. Neither car has an Australian-certified range or fuel figure.

Both claim class-leading charging, and Australian chargers cannot fully use either. The G9L's 800V platform is rated at **5C, 10–80% in about 11 minutes**, but that is a BEV figure; the EREV's peak rate is unconfirmed ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/09/17/xpeng-launches-g9l-china/); [Talk Android](https://www.talkandroid.com/533128-xpeng-g9l-europe-launch-price/)). The 8X's 70kWh pack claims **6C, 20–80% in about 9 minutes** and about 10.3 minutes for 10–80%, with a quoted **500kW peak** ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/04/17/zeekr-launches-8x-suv/); [MotorWatt](https://ev.motorwatt.com/ev-database/database-electric-cars/zeekr-8x)). Australia's fastest public units are **400kW ABB chargers** at only a handful of sites ([electrive](https://www.electrive.com/2026/02/02/abb-and-ampol-roll-out-first-400-kw-ultra-fast-chargers-in-australia/)), so the 8X's paper advantage will rarely show. Both still charge several times faster than PHEVs already on sale, such as the 74kW Sealion 8. The G9L adds confirmed **6kW vehicle-to-load** ([XPeng HK](https://xpeng.com.hk/g9l-spec)); the 8X's V2L output was not found.

## The G9L seats seven; the 8X pampers five

The two cars are almost the same size outside: the **G9L measures 5,120 × 1,999 × 1,788mm on a 3,100mm wheelbase** and the **8X 5,100 × 1,998 × 1,780mm on 3,069mm** ([XPeng UK](https://store.xpeng.com/gb/configurator/G9L_R); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeekr_8X)). They use that space very differently. The G9L offers **six or seven seats**, with seven expected as standard in Australia ([GoAuto](https://www.goauto.com.au/future-models/xpeng/g9/l/2026-10-13/100685.html)). Its boot holds **259L behind the third row, 935L with it folded, and a 65L frunk** ([autohled](https://www.autohled.cz/en/a/xpeng/g9l)). The 8X is listed as a **five-seater** ([autohled](https://www.autohled.cz/en/a/zeekr/8x)). One claim of a six-seat cabin could not be verified, and the six-seat model is the larger 9X ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeekr_9X)). WhichCar says the 8X's rear-seat focus comes "at the expense of boot space" ([WhichCar](https://www.whichcar.com.au/reviews/zeekr-8x-review-tech-rich-plug-in-hybrid-suv-with-massive-power)). Both are rated to tow **2,000kg braked**, although the G9L figure comes from the BEV ([EV Database](https://ev-database.org/car/3839/XPENG-G9L-RWD-Long-Range); [Carsauce](https://carsauce.com/news/zeekr-8x-confirmed-specs-revealed-for-new-plug-in-hybrid-suv)).

On luxury the 8X leads, though not by much on equipment. The G9L is generously specified, with **dual-chamber air suspension, heated, ventilated and massaging seats in the front and second rows, a 17.3-inch centre screen, a 21.4-inch rear entertainment screen and 23–33 speakers** ([Discover EV](https://www.discoverev.co.uk/ev-reviews/xpeng-g9l-review); [What Car?](https://www.whatcar.com/xpeng/g9l/suv/review)). Reviewers found its cabin "a little generic and minimalistic", with a tight front footwell ([ArenaEV](https://www.arenaev.com/xpeng_g9l__features_and_design_review-news-6306.php)). The 8X has **air suspension with active dampers, Naim audio (29 speakers with Dolby Atmos on Ultra trims and above), a 44-inch AR head-up display and Nappa seats that recline to 121 degrees** ([CarNewsChina](https://carnewschina.com/2026/03/09/zeekr-8x-interior-revealed-naim-audio-and-tech-inherited-from-9x-flagship/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeekr_8X)). Reviewers rate the finish highly. Auto Express ranked the cabin among the best on a Chinese car ([Auto Express](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/zeekr/369450/new-zeekr-8x-review-1381bhp-plug-hybrid-suv-absolute-tech-fest)), Carwow called it "Rolls Royce luxury for a fifth of the price" ([Carwow](https://www.carwow.co.uk/news/10704/zeekr-8x-review-mat-watson-2026)), and Drive scored it **8.1/10** after a short first drive ([Drive](https://www.drive.com.au/reviews/2026-zeekr-8x-review-international-first-drive/)). Australian trims of both cars are likely to have less equipment than the China flagships, and which features they keep is unknown.

Driver-assistance hardware is impressive on both, and in Australia both will likely ship with only basic functions. The G9L carries up to three in-house **Turing chips (2,250 TOPS)** ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/09/17/xpeng-launches-g9l-china/)). The 8X uses **NVIDIA Drive Thor-U plus LiDAR**, and the flagship Yaoying has two Thor chips and five LiDARs ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/04/17/zeekr-launches-8x-suv/)). Xpeng's NZ arm says its "advanced driving tech" will follow in 2027 ([AutoTrader NZ](https://autotrader.co.nz/showroom/xpeng/xpeng-confirms-five-model-rollout-for-new-zealand-advanced-driving-tech-to-follow-in-2027)). That suggests China-style city navigation-assisted driving will not be enabled at launch, and the same caution applies to the 8X. Expect standard adaptive cruise, lane-centring and AEB from both, and treat the chip count as future-proofing only.

## Xpeng's 66 sites and 7-year warranty beat Zeekr's thinner offer

Neither car has a crash rating from **ANCAP, Euro NCAP or C-NCAP**. Xpeng says the G9L was engineered to the 2027 Euro NCAP five-star standard ([What Car?](https://www.whatcar.com/xpeng/g9l/suv/review); [XPeng](https://www.xpeng.com/model/G9L)), and its G9 sibling scored five stars in 2023 ([Euro NCAP](https://www.euroncap.com/assessments/xpeng/g9/1048/)). Zeekr's Australian models, including the X and the 7X, have earned five ANCAP stars ([WhichCar](https://www.whichcar.com.au/?p=604738); [CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-news/top-marks-for-chinese-newcomers-2026-leapmotor-b10-and-zeekr-7x-among-electrified-cars-to)). On brand track record safety is a tie, and neither model is proven.

Ownership is where the G9L pulls ahead. **Xpeng Australia offers 7 years/unlimited km on the vehicle and 8 years/160,000km on the battery and drive unit** for cars sold from 1 June 2026 ([CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/full-steam-ahead-for-xpeng-as-it-expands-network-lineup-and-resolves-open-trueev-cases); [XPeng AU FAQ](https://www.xpeng.com/au/insight/FAQ)). It plans **66 sales and service locations across Australia and NZ by December 2026** ([The Driven](https://thedriven.io/2026/10/27/xpeng-expands-dealer-and-service-network-across-australia-and-new-zealand-ahead-of-new-electric-models/)). The distributor dispute flagged in the earlier report is largely over: TrueEV's Federal Court case was **dismissed in August 2026** after it failed to pay about $1.26m in security for costs ([CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/local-distributor-loses-australian-court-battle-with-chinas-xpeng)), and Xpeng is honouring TrueEV-era warranties ([Chasing Cars](https://www.chasingcars.com.au/news/car-industry/xpeng-australia-offers-aftersales-olive-branch-to-trueev-customers-as-distribution-dispute-ends/)). **Zeekr offers 5 years/unlimited km on the vehicle, 8 years/160,000km on the battery and 5 years of roadside assistance** ([Zeekr AU](https://www.zeekrlife.com/en-au/service)). Its network was targeted to pass **25 locations by the end of 2026** ([carsales](https://www.carsales.com.au/research/zeekr/7x/2026/)), up from 12 dealers in December 2025 ([CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/chinese-brands-claim-a-quarter-of-aussie-auto-market-but-accounting-firm-says-boom-wont-last)). Neither brand has said whether its range-extender or petrol engine falls under the longer powertrain cover. Ask that in writing, because the engine is the newest part of each brand's Australian service operation. Buyers outside the capital cities should also expect a long drive to the nearest Zeekr service point.

## Both lose the FBT exemption, so LCT and fuel use decide the tax bill

Tax treats both cars the same way. **New PHEVs have had no FBT exemption since 1 April 2025** ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/fbtphev)). Neither the 8X nor the 9X qualifies ([CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/zeekr-8x-plug-in-hybrid-suv-locked-in-for-australia)), and range-extenders such as the G9L EREV are treated as plug-in hybrids too ([zecar](https://zecar.com/reviews/xpeng-g9l-australia-q4-launch)). A novated lease on either car is therefore a normal taxed car benefit. The battery-electric G9L would be different, if it comes. From **1 April 2027**, EVs valued at $75,000 or less keep the full exemption, while those between $75k and the LCT threshold get only a **25% discount** ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com.au/tax/tax-alerts/government-announces-phased-changes-to-the-FBT-electric-car-exemption.html)). Because both cars arrive after that date, the April deadline highlighted in the earlier report does little for either. It only matters for a buyer comparing them with an EV that can be delivered before then, such as the 7X.

The **2026–27 LCT thresholds are $91,661 for fuel-efficient vehicles and $80,809 for the rest**, and a car counts as fuel-efficient at **3.5L/100km combined or less** ([ABF Customs Notice 2026-19](https://www.abf.gov.au/help-and-support-subsite/CustomsNotices/2026-19.pdf); [Chasing Cars](https://www.chasingcars.com.au/news/financing/changes-to-luxury-car-tax-for-2026-will-hit-toyota-kluger-mazda-cx-90-audi-q5-and-australian-family-suv-buyers-hardest/)). Neither car has an Australian ADR combined figure yet. With 250–330km of electric range, both should rate far below 3.5L/100km on the plug-in-weighted test, which would put them under the higher threshold. That is an inference to confirm on the Green Vehicle Guide at launch. In practice, a G9L at its targeted price would attract no LCT. An 8X priced above $91,661, which covers most of the media estimates for upper grades, would pay LCT on the amount over the threshold, adding to an already higher price.

Running costs depend on how often the car is plugged in. Home charging costs about **$5–7 per 100km against $12–15 for petrol** ([Solar Choice](https://www.solarchoice.net.au/ev/charging/cost-to-charge-ev-in-australia)), so either car used mainly for daily trips within its 250km-plus electric range costs close to an EV to run. On long trips the 8X's lower depleted-battery consumption (about 6.5L/100km against about 7.8) gives it the edge. EV insurance typically runs **0–40% above comparable petrol cars** ([Taylor Fry](https://taylorfry.com.au/articles/australias-ev-boom-how-insurers-need-to-respond/)), and a 660kW–1,030kW 8X is likely to sit at the top of that range. Depreciation is the largest unknown. There is no resale data for either brand, and young Chinese EVs have lost about half their value in two years ([The Nightly](https://thenightly.com.au/australia/why-evs-have-really-poor-resale-value-in-australia-tesla-byd-and-mg-prices-are-falling-faster-than-rivals-c-20112843)).

## Scorecard: the 8X edges it 7.9 to 7.4 on power-first weights

The weights follow the buyer's stated priorities: power first, then luxury, features, range, fast charging and value. Practicality and ownership are added because a car this size is usually bought for a family. Scores are out of 10 and are my judgement from the China and Europe specs cited above. They will need revising once Australian specs and prices are announced.

| Category (weight) | Xpeng G9L | Zeekr 8X | Winner and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Power and acceleration (25%) | 6 | 10 | **8X**: 660–1,030kW against ~370kW EREV; 2.96s against 4.95s |
| Luxury and cabin (15%) | 7 | 9 | **8X**: Naim audio, AR HUD, rated among the best Chinese cabins; G9L is well equipped but "generic" |
| Features, tech and ADAS (10%) | 8 | 8 | **Tie**: G9L has rear-steer, a rear screen and 2,250 TOPS; 8X has LiDAR and Thor. Both likely limited in AU |
| Range and efficiency (10%) | 8 | 8 | **Tie**: similar EV range (287km against 256–328km); G9L has the longer combined claim, 8X uses less fuel |
| Fast charging (10%) | 8 | 9 | **8X** narrowly: 6C/500kW against 5C, though both are capped by 400kW AU chargers |
| Value and price certainty (15%) | 8 | 5 | **G9L**: $75–90k target and cheaper in China; 8X estimates run to $135k |
| Size, seats and practicality (5%) | 9 | 6 | **G9L**: seven seats, larger boot, frunk |
| Warranty, network, safety, timing (10%) | 8 | 5 | **G9L**: 7yr against 5yr, 66 against ~25 sites, arrives ~6 months earlier |

The result depends heavily on the power weighting. Cut power to 15% and move that weight to value and practicality, and the G9L wins. If the cheapest Australian 8X lands above about $95k drive-away, it falls outside the budget and the comparison ends there.

## Verdict by buyer type, and how this changes the earlier picks

**Overall**: the 8X is the more desirable car, and the G9L is the one most buyers on this budget will end up with. **A family that needs seven seats should choose the G9L.** The 8X is a five-seater, so the comparison is settled before power comes into it. Wait the few weeks for Xpeng's pricing, which is due before the end of 2026, and compare it against the BYD Sealion 8 Premium AWD, the earlier report's seven-seat PHEV pick. The G9L offers similar power (370kW against 359kW), much faster charging (5C against 74kW), a longer warranty and more luxury, so it should replace the Sealion 8 as the three-row pick if it is priced near $85k. **A power-first driver who can wait until late 2027** should target the 8X, but only once Zeekr publishes a price and confirms that a dual-motor 660kW grade fits under $90k drive-away. **A power-first driver who wants a car in the next six months** should choose neither. The **Zeekr 7X Performance AWD**, the earlier report's number-one pick, has **500kW for $72,900 before on-roads** and is delivering from December 2026 ([Zecar](https://zecar.com/reviews/2027-zeekr-7x-update-australia-more-power-same-price)). It has more power than the G9L, a five-star ANCAP rating, and, as a BEV, access to the FBT exemption if it is leased before 1 April 2027. **A buyer who wants long-distance range without public charging** suits either car. The 8X uses less fuel on the highway, while the G9L has the longer claimed combined range and the bigger network.

On whether to wait: **waiting costs little for G9L pricing and a lot for the 8X**. The G9L's price is due within weeks and its arrival is about three months away. Before committing to the 8X you would wait about nine to twelve months, and its price could land anywhere from $80k to $135k. Launch dates have already slipped for both cars, and the 8X's has moved from "this summer" to H2 2027. Treat the 8X as a car to reassess when Zeekr announces Australian pricing, not one to plan a purchase around today. One caveat applies to the whole comparison: the research relied on search-result summaries because direct page fetches from Drive, carsales, CarsGuide, EV Central and CnEVPost were blocked. Several figures conflict across sources, including the G9L EREV's 370kW against 405kW and the 8X's seat count. Confirm each against Australian specifications when they are published.

## Conclusion

This comparison is less about which car is better than about how much an extra 300–600kW is worth to the buyer. The 8X delivers that power and a better cabin, but Zeekr's China pricing implies a higher local price and its later arrival implies a long wait. Since both cars lose the FBT exemption as plug-in hybrids, the usual reason to rush a novated lease does not apply, which is why waiting for the 8X is viable at all. Even so, waiting means paying more for power that ordinary roads rarely use, while the G9L's real strengths (seven seats, a 7-year warranty and a network nearly three times Zeekr's) are useful every day.

The larger change from the earlier report is that the three-row category has caught up with the five-seat EVs. A 5C-charging, air-sprung, seven-seat range-extender targeted under $90k would have been unthinkable a year ago, and it makes the current seven-seat PHEVs look dated. For the five-seat power buyer, the earlier conclusion still holds and is if anything stronger: the 7X Performance delivers most of the 8X's appeal now, with a confirmed price, tax advantages and a five-star rating, about a year before the 8X arrives.
